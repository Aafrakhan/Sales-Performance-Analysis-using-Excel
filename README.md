# Sales Performance Analysis

An interactive Excel dashboard that turns one year of sales data into clear answers for business questions, with insights and recommendations for the business.

---

## Project Overview

**Business problem:** A small business sells seven products (Backpack, Chair, Charger, Desk Lamp, Notebook, Pen Set and USB Drive) across four regions (East, North, South and West). The sales data covers a full year, but it was hard to see how the business was performing, which months, regions and products matter most, and where to act.

**Objective:** Build one dashboard that shows full-year sales performance and answers the key business questions: when sales and orders are highest, which regions and products lead, who the top customers are, and what caused the biggest sales drop.

---

## Dataset

| Detail | Value |
|---|---|
| Period | 12 months (one full year) |
| Orders | 500 |
| Units sold | 5,314 |
| Customers | 206 |
| Products | 7 |
| Regions | 4 |

**Main fields used:** Order ID, Customer, Month, Region, Item Category, Quantity, Sales Amount, Cost and Profit.

---

## Tools and Skills

- **Microsoft Excel:** Pivot Tables, Pivot Charts, Slicers, KPI cards
- **Dashboard design:** layout, colour theme, sorted charts, clear titles
- **Business analysis:** turning questions into charts, insights and recommendations
- **Data storytelling:** Question, Answer, Insight, Recommendation

---

## Dashboard Features

- **6 KPI cards:** Total Revenue, Total Quantity Sold, Total Orders, Average Order Value, Total Customers and Total Profit
- **Charts:** Monthly Sales Trend, Orders over Time, Sales by Region, Profit by Region, Top 5 Customers by Revenue, Sales vs Cost by Top 5 Product, Profit by Top 5 Category and Quantity Sold by Top 5 Product
- **4 slicers:** Month, Region, Item Category and Quarter, so anyone can drill into a month, region, product or quarter

## Deep Dive: What Caused the June Drop?

Using the Month slicer, May and June were compared:

| | May | June | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales | $166k | $89k | -46% |
| Orders | 47 | 32 | -32% |
| Average order value | about $3.5k | about $2.8k | -21% |
| East sales | $54k | $11k | -80% |
| South sales | $49k | $29k | -41% |
| West sales | $46k | $33k | -28% |
| North sales | $16k | $16k | flat |
| Chair sales | $50k | $10k | -80% |

**What this shows:**
- East caused more than half of the $77k drop.
- Chair, the top product, fell from $50k to $10k, and Backpack dropped out of the Top 5.
- Both the number of orders and the value of each order went down.
- Chair units fell from 145 to 57, but its sales fell faster, so each unit brought in less money.

---

## Key Insights

- East and Chair are the biggest drivers of sales.
- Sales were strongest in the first half of the year, with a peak in May and a sharp drop in June.
- The June drop came mainly from East and Chair, and from both fewer and smaller orders.
- More orders do not always mean more sales. December shows this clearly.
- The Top 5 products bring in about 86% of revenue.
- Revenue is spread across 206 customers, so the business does not depend on a few buyers.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Protect East and Chair.** Keep Chair in stock and support East with campaigns and stock planning.
2. **Investigate June in East.** Check stock, customer activity and Chair pricing, and plan a June campaign.
3. **Plan for the second half.** Repeat what worked in Q2 and prepare a stronger year-end push.
4. **Raise order value.** Use bundles and upselling in low-value months, especially December and February.
5. **Lift orders in slow months.** Run offers in June and October.
6. **Reward loyal customers.** Create a loyalty offer for top customers and encourage repeat orders from the rest.
